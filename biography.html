Chia Webb-Cázares is a queer, Chicanx painter, illustrator, mosaicist, sculptor, and tattoo artist from Austin, Texas. Their visual art explores themes of childhood, heritage, botany, queerness, magic, spirituality, God, and self-actualization. As a 4th-generation Mexican East Austinite, bearing witness to its rapid gentrification was a catalyst for their work as an outlet for grieving lost community. Chia’s work communicates their desire to preserve their literal and symbolic “home.”


In 2019, Chia attended Mount Holyoke College to major in Visual Art, but in 2021, they withdrew to follow in the footsteps of their entrepreneurial parents, beginning their primarily self-taught career as a tattoo artist. Since then, they have solidified themself as a full-time tattoo artist in Austin, Texas, and beyond, having worked nationally in Los Angeles, New York City, Portland, and Seattle, and internationally in Rio de Janeiro, London, Amsterdam, Paris, Berlin, and Lisbon. Their tattoo work is heavily influenced by botanical imagery and a connection to the land, fostered by their herbalist mother and brujo father.


Alongside their ongoing tattoo practice, Chia works as a muralist for the Green & White Grocery, a historic botánica in East Austin. In 2024, they restored a 3 foot wide 1950’s paper mache Quetzalcoatl sculpture passed down to the store through the local Latino restaurant community. The sculpture is mounted in the botánica as the centerpiece of a Mesoamerican codex-inspired mural that Chia is currently completing. They have also worked as an assistant to renowned Austin public mural artist Federico Archuleta, aiding him in restoring his spray paint mural at the Green & White Grocery and completing a mural commissioned by Spec’s Wine, Spirits & Finer Foods spanning the entire length of the building. In February 2024, Chia exhibited a portrait in the Mix n’ Mash group exhibition at Mexic-Arte Museum in Austin, TX. Since 2024, they have completed various commissioned oil portraits and, in July 2026, began working as a visual art tutor. In October 2027, Chia will be an artist in residence at Arquetopia Foundation, the award-winning international arts and cultural nonprofit in Puebla, Mexico.


Though they’ve had a relationship with painting as long as they can remember–honored by their adoring mother laminating their 2002 finger paintings–Chia had their first introduction to oil painting in 2024. Quickly enamored with the medium, their newfound practice culminated in their January 2026 debut collection, God Undercover, displayed in conjunction with multi-media artwork from three of their closest friends and collaborators in a successful group exhibition called Mollisol. The event drew around 250 attendees and was entirely self-produced by the four young artists, including conception, installation, outreach to curators, buyers, artists, and community members, and securing an event space, drinks sponsors, volunteers, a photographer, and live music, all on a very small budget.
